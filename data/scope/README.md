# data/scope: scope set, lexicon and probes for the input gate (M5G)

Written under `evals/protocols/m5g-gate.md` (SPEC D103 to D118, D126, D145, D150). Owned by M5G.
Everything here is fictional: no real company, person, product or URL, no demo-company names.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `scope_set.jsonl` | 360 owner messages, one JSON object per line: `sid`, `text`, `label` (`in_scope` or `out_of_scope`), `style`, `recent_turns` (zero to two prior turns, each `{role, text}` with role `owner` or `assistant`), `split` (`dev` or `test`). No `session_title` (D145). |
| `lexicon.yaml` | Stage-1 term lists (D106): module ids and names, integration ids, the 25 protocol field ids with two to five synonyms each, knowledge-base titles, trade words from the demo companies, session and account action words, the greeting list, number-unit words, and the narrow refuse patterns for rule 4. Every term lower-case; short forms sit next to their full form. `version: scope-lexicon.v1`. |
| `probes.jsonl` | 80 probe rows, separate from the scope set: `sid`, `category`, `text`, `recent_turns`, `expected` (`pass`, `refuse` or `band`). The two `empty` rows carry a `note`: they never reach the gate (`validate` rejects them, SPEC section 8), so `expected` records that rejection, not a gate verdict. |
| `scope-audit.md` | Not yet written. The auditing session agent adds it on `agent/audit/m5g`. |

## Counts achieved

- Labels and splits: in-scope 240 (120 dev, 120 test), out-of-scope 120 (60 dev, 60 test). Split alternates
  dev, test within each (label, style) group, so every style is split evenly.
- In-scope styles: short 40, long 30, typo 40, fragment 30, multi_part 30, follow_up 30, greeting_business 20,
  session_action 20. Out-of-scope styles: chat 24, creative 16, code_homework 16, unrelated_advice 16,
  personal_to_assistant 12, rule_change 16, other_products 12, other 8.
- Non-English rows (Spanish, German, Korean): in-scope 10 dev and 8 test, out-of-scope 5 dev and 5 test
  (caps 10 and 5 per split). 28 rows in total.
- Every follow_up row has one or two `recent_turns`; 7 out-of-scope rows also carry in-scope context so
  that context alone does not predict the label.
- Topic spread (in-scope rows matching a lexicon group, text plus context): trade words 101,
  protocol fields 81, knowledge-base titles 67, session and account actions 38, integrations 32,
  modules 26. All 6 modules and all 25 protocol fields are touched by at least one row.
- Probes per category: clear_in_scope 9, clear_off_topic 9, ambiguous 8, negation 6, numbers_dates 7,
  follow_up 8, injected_instruction 8, mixed 8, non_english 7, empty 2, very_short 8.

## Labelling rules applied

A pure greeting is `in_scope` (D104, fixed reply). A message that mixes one in-scope question or fact with
off-topic text is `in_scope`. A rule-change or persona request with no in-scope part is `out_of_scope`.
Follow-ups are decidable from their `recent_turns`. Labels were fixed before any probe (D107).

## Checks run before hand-over

Counts per label, split and style against the protocol table; no duplicate text after lower-casing and
whitespace collapse (also none between probes and scope rows); no pair with word-set Jaccard above 0.6;
no D22 phrase in any in-scope row (text or context); no demo-company or demo-owner name; short rows under
8 words, long rows 40 to 120 words; lexicon loads with `yaml.safe_load`, all terms lower-case, 2 to 5
synonyms per field; a simulation of stage-1 rules 1 to 5 against this lexicon refuses no in-scope row and
passes no out-of-scope row (so every out-of-scope row is either refused by rule 4 or reaches stage 2).
That last result is by construction (the same agent wrote the lexicon and the rows) and says nothing about
bare-word collisions in the wild ("job", "order", "staff"). Under this lexicon about 32 dev in-scope rows
and 47 dev out-of-scope rows reach stage 2, so the D115 thresholds are order statistics over those rows,
not over all 120. Cross-split pairs were also listed (stop-word-free Jaccard at least 0.3) and reworded
where one side was a rewording of the other. Probes repeat "yes" and "ok go on" on purpose: once with
context (expected `pass`) and once without (`band`).
