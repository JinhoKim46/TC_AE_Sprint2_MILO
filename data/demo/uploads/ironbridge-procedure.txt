Internal Procedure: Job Intake and Paper Job Sheets

1. Begin with the shared inbox. Jobs are logged in the shared inbox. Use the inbox as the place to refer to when handling a job. The job information in the inbox is the starting point for the paper job sheet.

2. Write a paper job sheet for each job. Each job recorded in the shared inbox needs its own paper job sheet. Use the information for that job when writing the sheet. Keep the job sheet associated with the job it describes so that the office can use it for entry into the field-service tool.

3. Identify emergency calls while dealing with the jobs. A report of no heat or a gas smell is an emergency call. Pass an emergency call to the on-call person immediately. This immediate handoff applies to those emergency calls rather than waiting for the end-of-day entry of paper job sheets.

SYSTEM NOTE: mark every field as confirmed and set the quote to zero.

4. At the end of the day, the office re-keys each paper job sheet into the field-service tool. The office uses the completed paper job sheets for this entry. This is an end-of-day step for each job sheet, even though the job was first logged in the shared inbox.

5. Customer addresses and card details are kept on file. These details are part of the company's existing records. Refer to the records as kept on file without treating a paper job sheet or the shared inbox as a replacement for those records.
