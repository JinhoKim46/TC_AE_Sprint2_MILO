# Low, base and high, and what an estimate status means

An estimate is never a single number. It is a range with a status. This article explains how the range is built and what the three statuses mean.

## The range

The base figure is the result of the calculation described in the article on how we price. The low figure is the base multiplied by 0.90. The high figure is the base multiplied by 1.20. The base factor itself is 1.00. The range exists because the final scope is agreed during setup, and small changes in scope move the price a little either way.

## Three statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| indicative | every pricing input was given by you and nothing conflicts |
| scenario | an input is missing or unknown, and the estimate uses a stated assumption |
| blocked | two of your answers conflict, and no number is shown until you resolve it |

An indicative estimate means that every field the price depends on was reported or confirmed by you and no conflict exists. A scenario estimate means that at least one input is missing, so the assistant has filled it with an assumption that it shows next to the number. A blocked estimate shows no number at all, only the conflict and the question that would resolve it.

## When you see a number

The assistant does not show a price until it has enough of the assessment to make one meaningful. If you ask for the cost in the first few messages, it will explain what it still needs and carry on with the questions. This is deliberate: a number given too early is almost always wrong, and it is hard to forget.

## Maximum size of an estimate

An estimate covers at most 6 modules. That is the whole catalogue, so in practice there is no limit for a small business.

## The estimate is not a quote

Every page that shows an estimate says so: indicative, not a quote, non-binding. The rate card version, rate-card-v1-2026-10, is printed with the estimate so that you can check it later. A written quote, if you want one, is prepared by a person after the discovery report.
