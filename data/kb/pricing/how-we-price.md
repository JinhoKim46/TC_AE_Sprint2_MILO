# How we price

MILO prices are arithmetic, not negotiation. Every estimate is built from a published rate card, and the same inputs always give the same number. This article explains the parts of the calculation so that you can check any estimate yourself.

## The rate card

The current rate card is rate-card-v1-2026-10 and every price on it is in USD. It lists a base monthly price for each module, one setup fee per module, a monthly surcharge for each kind of integration, a set of volume tiers, and the factors that turn one number into a low and a high figure.

## The four inputs

An estimate needs four things: which modules you want, how many requests you handle on a normal day, which integrations your tools offer, and how many modules you combine. The discovery assistant collects these from you in conversation. Nothing else changes the price.

## The calculation

The monthly price for each chosen module is its base price multiplied by the volume factor for your daily request volume. The integration surcharges for the integrations you use are added once a month. The setup fee is 900 USD for each module and is paid once. The result is the base estimate, from which the low and high figures are derived.

## What the assistant may and may not do

The assistant may suggest which catalogue modules fit what you described. It cannot change the arithmetic, apply a discount, or set a price itself. If you see a number in the conversation that is not in the estimate panel, treat the panel as the only valid figure.

## Why we show it this way

Owners told us that the thing they distrust most about automation quotes is not knowing how the number was reached. A published rate card and a visible calculation mean that an estimate can be checked line by line before anyone commits.
