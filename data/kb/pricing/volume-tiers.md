# Volume tiers

Your daily request volume changes the monthly price of every module you choose. The change is a multiplier, called the volume factor, taken from a short table. This article shows the table and how to read it.

## The table

| Daily requests | Volume factor |
|---|---|
| up to 20 per day | 1.00 |
| up to 60 per day | 1.25 |
| up to 150 per day | 1.50 |
| above 150 per day | no automatic estimate; a person prices it |

## How to read it

Find the row that your normal daily volume falls into, and multiply each module's base price by the factor in that row. A business with volume up to 20 requests a day pays the base price with no change. A business with volume up to 60 requests a day pays the base price multiplied by 1.25. A business with volume up to 150 requests a day pays the base price multiplied by 1.50.

## Above the top tier

Above 150 requests per day there is no automatic estimate and a person prices it. The assistant will tell you that the number is outside the rate card and that someone will contact you, rather than showing a figure.

## Which number counts

The volume that counts is the number of requests on a normal business day. If you know your volume per month, say so, and the assistant converts it to a daily figure in code and shows you the conversion. If you give two figures that do not agree, for example a daily number and a monthly number that imply different volumes, the assistant will ask you which one to use. It does not pick one for you, and no estimate is shown until the conflict is resolved.

## Why volume matters

The modules do more work when more requests come in: more calls answered, more messages labelled, more tickets checked for duplicates. The tiers keep the price proportionate without pricing each request one by one.
