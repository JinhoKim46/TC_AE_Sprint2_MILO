# Volume and timing questions

The questions about volume and minutes are the ones that owners find hardest to answer precisely. This article explains what we ask, why a rough answer is fine, and what happens when two answers disagree.

## The volume question

We ask roughly how many requests come in on a normal day. A request is anything that needs handling: a call, a message, a form, a walk-in. A normal day means a typical business day, not the busiest one and not the quietest. If you think in weeks or months, say so; the assistant converts the figure to a daily one in code and shows the conversion as an estimated fact.

## The minutes questions

We ask two minutes figures. The first is how long one request takes to handle, from picking up the call or opening the message to having what you need. The second is how many minutes of typing or re-keying follow, for example writing the booking into a spreadsheet or entering a job sheet into a tool. Both are used in the simple return estimate in the report, and both are labelled as reported so you can see where the number came from.

## Why a rough answer is enough

The volume only has to land in the right tier of the rate card, and the tiers are wide. The minutes figures are used to estimate hours per month, and a small error in minutes makes a small error in hours. A rough honest figure is more useful than a precise guess.

## When two answers disagree

If you give a daily figure at one point and a monthly figure later, and the two imply different volumes, the assistant keeps both and marks the fact as conflicting. It then asks which figure reflects a normal month. No estimate is shown until you answer, because a price built on the wrong volume would be in the wrong tier. The assistant does not quietly choose the later answer.

## Timing

We ask when you would want to start. If you have not decided, say so. The estimate is then marked as a scenario, with the undecided start shown as an assumption, rather than as an indicative figure. A start date does not change the price; it changes how firm the estimate can be.

## Budget

We ask whether you have a budget range in mind, and it is fine to say you do not know. The budget is recorded for the report and is never used to set the price; the price comes from the rate card alone.
