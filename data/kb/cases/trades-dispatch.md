# Case: a heating firm gets jobs out of the inbox

This is an anonymised, fictional case. The firm, its staff and its figures are invented.

## The situation

A heating and plumbing firm with several vans received most of its jobs in a shared inbox. The office read the mail each morning, wrote a paper job sheet for each job, and handed the sheets to the engineers. At the end of the day the sheets were re-keyed into a field-service tool that could export data but could not import it. Jobs were lost in the inbox when mail arrived late in the day, re-keying produced wrong addresses, and night calls about no heat or a gas smell reached no one until morning.

## What the discovery found

The interview recorded the channels, the volume, the minutes per job and the re-keying minutes. The owner gave two volumes that did not agree, a daily figure and a monthly figure, and the assistant raised the conflict instead of choosing one. The estimate stayed blocked until the owner said which figure reflected a normal month. The owner also confirmed that urgent requests existed and that customer addresses and card details were kept on file, so the report carried a personal data note.

## What was recommended

Email triage to label and prioritise the inbox, ticket-creation so that each accepted job became a tracked ticket with a duplicate check, staff-assignment to suggest which engineer should take a ticket from the skill tags the owner entered, and document-data-entry to turn the paper job sheets into a checked list for re-keying. Because the field-service tool was export-only, the report said plainly that re-keying could be shortened and checked but not removed, and the export-only-tool surcharge was shown. That surcharge applied to document-data-entry; ticket-creation and staff-assignment used csv-export with a spreadsheet, because the field-service tool could not import their output.

## What changed

Mail was labelled as it arrived, so a job sent late in the day was visible the same evening. Each job had a ticket with an owner and a status, kept in a spreadsheet fed by the CSV file rather than in the field-service tool. The night line was routed to the on-call engineer through the urgent label. The paper sheets stayed, but they were printed from the ticket rather than written by hand.

## What it did not do

The modules did not replace the field-service tool and did not make the re-keying disappear. The estimate was a scenario, because the owner had not decided when to start, and the budget was recorded as unknown.

## Why it is a useful case

It shows two things the assistant must do well: keep a conflict visible rather than overwrite it, and say when an integration limit means a goal cannot be fully met.
