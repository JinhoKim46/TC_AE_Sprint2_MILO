# Case: a grooming salon stops missing bookings

This is an anonymised, fictional case. The owner, the salon and the figures are invented to show how a discovery interview turns into a working module.

## The situation

A small pet grooming salon with two sites took bookings by phone and through a web form. Every booking was written into a shared spreadsheet by whoever answered, and reminders were sent by hand the day before. At peak times the phone rang while every groomer had a dog on the table, so calls went to voicemail and some were never returned. The owner's words in the interview were that the business lost money twice: once when a call was missed, and again when a customer forgot and did not show.

## What the discovery found

The interview recorded the channels, the daily volume, the minutes per call and the minutes of spreadsheet entry, each with the owner's own sentence as evidence. The pain points were missed calls, reminders sent by hand, and no-shows. The errors were missed and duplicated bookings. The booking widget could export CSV, so the csv-export integration was available with no surcharge.

## What was recommended

Two modules: phone-intake, to answer calls at peak times and out of hours and to book into open slots, and customer-status-updates, to send the reminder before each appointment from an approved template. The estimate was marked indicative because every pricing input had been reported and nothing conflicted.

## What changed

Calls at peak times were answered and recorded, with a transcript for each. Reminders went out from the template without anyone typing them. The spreadsheet stayed in use, fed by the CSV file, so the staff did not have to learn a new tool. The owner kept approving refunds by hand, which was the only approval step in the process.

## What it did not do

The modules did not stop every no-show, and the report did not promise that they would. The salon still decided its own cancellation policy. Card payments by phone were out of scope for phone-intake, so deposits stayed a manual step.

## Why it worked

The process was simple and the rules were clear: a call becomes a booking, a booking gets a reminder. That clarity is what made the case a good fit, and it is the first thing the discovery interview looks for.
